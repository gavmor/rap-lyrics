# Rap Styles Reference Guide: The Deconstructed Masters

This guide provides deep technical blueprints for the 8 distinct rapping styles highlighted in the Vox Earworm documentary *"Rapping, deconstructed: The best rhymers of all time"* (featuring musicologist Martin Connor, Estelle Caswell, and Open Mike Eagle).

---

## 1. Kurtis Blow & The Old School Foundation (1980)
**Key Song:** *"The Breaks"* (1980)  
**Historical Role:** The commercial baseline of hip-hop before rhythmic complexity evolved.

### Structural Blueprint
- **Rhyme Scheme:** Strict AA BB CC DD couplets.
- **Rhyme Placement:** Rhymes land almost exclusively on beat 4 at the end of each measure.
- **Syntax vs. Barline:** 1-to-1 sentence-to-bar correspondence. Each sentence or independent clause starts on beat 1 and ends on beat 4. No enjambment or spillover.
- **Rhyme Type:** Monosyllabic (single-syllable) end rhymes (e.g., *breaks / takes*, *play / day*).
- **Complexity:** Minimal internal rhymes, wordplay, or metric syncopation. Predictable, nursery-rhyme-like metric regularity.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Keep the cadence steady, 4/4 meter, bouncing on the downbeats.
2. Put the primary rhyming word squarely on the 4th beat of every line.
3. Avoid multisyllabic rhymes or internal rhymes. Keep the syntax simple, punchy, and dance-oriented.
4. Keep themes festive, celebratory, or cautionary in a straightforward narrative tone.

## 2. Rakim: The Golden Age Architect (1986)
**Key Song:** *"Eric B. Is President"* (1986)  
**Historical Role:** Pioneered the internal rhyme, multisyllabic rhyme, the 16-dot bar grid, and crossing the bar line.

### Structural Blueprint
- **The 16-Dot Grid:** Rakim visualized a 4-bar section as a graph of 16 beats (or 64 16th-note subdivisions), placing words and syllables into every available pocket until "no other words could fit."
- **Internal Rhymes:** Placing rhyming syllables inside the line/sentence well before the bar line (e.g., *"I came in the door, I said it before"* or *"Indeed... Proceed"*).
- **Multisyllabic (Compound) Rhymes:** Rhyming phrases with matching consonant-vowel combinations across multiple syllables (e.g., *"man-made a mix"* / *"band-aid to fix"*).
- **Crossing the Bar Line (Enjambment):** The sentence does not stop at the bar line. A phrase begins in Bar 1, and its climax or second rhyming syllable lands squarely on **Beat 1 of Bar 2** (e.g., *"The rhyme can't be kept in- / [Bar 2, Beat 1] -side"*).
- **Metatheoretical Self-Awareness:** Rakim frequently describes his technical, physical, and mental writing process within the lyrics themselves.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Map out a 16th-note grid, budgeting **10 to 13 syllables per bar**. Leave space around Beat 2 and Beat 4 for the snare to breathe.
2. Formulate 3-to-4-syllable compound rhymes that match vowel and consonant textures without bloating the meter.
3. Intentionally carry a clause over the end of the bar so that the resolving primary syllable drops on Beat 1 of the next measure. **Crucial:** Never split across an unstressed reduced prefix (like *con-fined*); only split words/phrases with full vowel weight (like *in- / -side* or *mind- / -state*).
4. Avoid clunky 5-syllable polysyllables (e.g., *subdivisions*); use crisp, punchy words (*grid*, *dots*, *bars*, *fractions*, *cadence*) that fit natural conversational cadence.
5. Infuse lyrics with intellectual authority, smooth jazz-cadenced delivery, and references to the pen, the mic, and the craft.

## 3. The Notorious B.I.G.: The Smooth Interlocker (1997)
**Key Song:** *"Hypnotize"* (1997)  
**Historical Role:** Master of conversational flow, elastic sentence length, and daisy-chained rhyme schemes.

### Structural Blueprint
- **Daisy Chaining:** Interlocking rhyme schemes so that before one rhyme sound concludes, a new rhyme sound is introduced and woven into the phrase. Sound A links sentence 1 and 2; sentence 2 introduces Sound B; sentence 3 continues Sound B while introducing Sound C.
- **Rhythmic Unpredictability & Phrase Length Variety:** Alternating between brief, staccato statements (2 to 4 syllables) and sweeping, multi-bar legato runs (16+ syllables).
- **Effortless Barline Traversal:** Gliding across bar boundaries conversationally without sounding rushed.
- **Melodic and Rhythmic Motives:** Repeating a specific rhythmic bounce or melodic inflection across consecutive bars to create an irresistible vocal groove.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Chain your rhymes: Start with Scheme A (e.g., *-oo* sound: *crew / through*), then before ending that clause, introduce Scheme B (e.g., *-ih* sound: *fit / hit*), then pivot to Scheme B and introduce Scheme C.
2. Vary sentence length dramatically: Follow a rapid, 3-word punch with an expansive 18-syllable narrative run.
3. Blend street bravado, lavish humor, luxury, and conversational ease.

## 4. Mos Def (Yasiin Bey): The Metric Saturator (2002)
**Key Song:** *"Re:Definition"* (Black Star, 2002)  
**Historical Role:** Metric saturation—hitting nearly every subdivision within the bar with multisyllabic rhymes sustained over long passages.

### Structural Blueprint
- **Metric Saturation:** Filling nearly all 16 subdivisions of the bar with articulate syllables locked precisely to the tempo.
- **Sustained Multisyllabic Quad-Rhymes:** Maintaining a 4-syllable rhyme scheme consistently across 8, 12, or even 14 straight bars without losing thematic coherence.
- **On-the-Beat Precision:** Syllables hit downbeats and upbeats with drum-machine-like accuracy, creating a relentless, hypnotic cadence.
- **High Information Density:** Dense socio-political, philosophical, and urban cultural imagery compressed into every bar.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Choose a tight 4-syllable vowel-consonant anchor (e.g., Short-O / Long-A / Short-I / Long-EE pattern).
2. Lock every word to the drum pulse—no sloppy syllables, every vowel lands on a subdivision.
3. Sustain that exact 4-syllable pattern across at least 8 to 12 consecutive bars.
4. Weave deep consciousness, hip-hop history, Brooklyn street corner wisdom, and poetic elevation.

## 5. André 3000: The Syncopated Jazz Innovator (1998)
**Key Song:** *"Aquemini"* (OutKast, 1998)  
**Historical Role:** Metric displacement—accenting rhymes on shifting, offbeat subdivisions rather than predictable downbeats.

### Structural Blueprint
- **Metric Displacement / Offbeat Accents:** While most rappers place rhymes on predictable downbeats (e.g., beats 2 and 4, or standard 16th subdivisions like *dollars, parlors, bottles*), André shifts the accent to unexpected offbeats (the "e", the "&", or the "a" of the beat).
- **Syncopated, Asymmetrical Phrasing:** Phrases that breathe like a saxophone solo—unexpected pauses, triplet lurches, lingering vowels, and sudden bursts.
- **Introspective & Cosmic Storytelling:** Deep human empathy, existential reflection, mortality, Southern mysticism, and vivid street portraiture.
- **Conversational Elasticity:** Flowing like spoken-word jazz poetry that nevertheless locks firmly into the pocket.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Avoid placing rhymes on standard downbeats (beats 2 and 4). Shift the rhyme to the "and" of beat 2 or the "a" of beat 3.
2. Break symmetry: Follow a 7-syllable off-kilter phrase with a pause, then an eccentric 11-syllable reflection.
3. Use philosophical inquiries, introspective admissions, and vivid acoustic imagery (rain, stars, old cars, vinyl, Southern humidity).

## 6. Eminem: The Syllabic Storyteller & Vowel Bender (2002)
**Key Songs:** *"Business"* (2002), *"Lose Yourself"* (2002)  
**Historical Role:** Vowel bending (slant rhymes) and syllable-by-syllable phonetic deconstruction paired with urgent cinematic narrative.

### Structural Blueprint
- **Slant Rhyme & Vowel Bending:** Refusing phonetic limitations (e.g., rhyming "orange" by bending syllables to match *"door hinge"*, *"storage"*, *"porridge"*, *"four-inch"*). Modulating accents and vowel lengths to force non-rhyming words into harmonious assonance.
- **Syllable-Level Deconstruction:** Every bar is broken down into vowel clusters (e.g., short *a*, long *o*, short *i*), creating multi-tiered, overlapping internal and end rhymes.
- **Cinematic Narrative Stakes:** Uncompromising narrative momentum—sensory details (sweat, cold, shaky knees, ticking clocks, backstage corridors), psychological urgency, and high emotional stakes sustained through intricate rhyming matrices.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Pick words that don't traditionally rhyme and bend their vowel colors through deliberate phonetic spelling or cadence.
2. Layer multiple vowel tracks: Track 1 (e.g., *a* sounds) and Track 2 (e.g., *o* sounds) running simultaneously across 4 bars.
3. Keep the emotional temperature at boiling point: visceral urgency, adrenaline, high stakes, and sensory realism.

## 7. Kendrick Lamar: The Motivic Composer (2011)
**Key Song:** *"Rigamortus"* (2011)  
**Historical Role:** Compositional rap using musical motives, motivic daisy-chaining, and gear shifts.

### Structural Blueprint
- **The Musical Motive:** Introducing a distinct rhythmic motif (like Beethoven's 5th Symphony *short-short-short-long*). In *"Rigamortus"*, Kendrick uses a *short-short-long* rhythmic cell (e.g., two sixteenth notes followed by an eighth/quarter note: *"dead and gone"*, *"head is gone"*, *"let it burn"*).
- **Motivic Anchor:** The rhythmic motive repeats and evolves through the entire verse, serving as the musical glue holding the narrative together.
- **Shifting Gears:** Modulating metric speed—accelerating from 2nd gear (laid-back flow) to 3rd gear (rapid delivery) to "4th gear" (blistering double-time or triplets) while preserving the motivic rhythm to stay disciplined.
- **Breath Control & Vocal Dynamics:** Building dynamic intensity and pitch inflection as the verse progresses toward a breathless climax.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Establish a distinct 3-to-4 note rhythmic motif in Bar 1 (e.g., *da-da-DUM*).
2. Repeat and vary this motif across subsequent bars, anchoring different rhyme families to the same rhythmic shape.
3. Shift gears after bar 4 or 8: double the syllable rate into 32nd notes or triplet bursts, but maintain the motif's cadence at key resolution points.
4. Infuse prophetic, competitive, and culturally conscious themes with vocal dynamics.

## 8. MF DOOM: The Supervillain Holorimer (2004)
**Key Song:** *"Madvillainy"* (e.g., *"Meat Grinder"*, *"Accordion"*, 2004)  
**Historical Role:** The holorime (whole lines rhyming syllable-for-syllable), subversive punchlines, and oblique associative logic.

### Structural Blueprint
- **The Holorime:** Entire bars rhyming with each other syllable for syllable. The setup bar matches every vowel and consonant in the punchline bar.
- **Subverting Expectations:** Leading the listener down a predictable lyrical path and pulling the rug out at the last second. When rap convention expects a cliché (e.g., expecting *"bitches"*), DOOM pivots hilariously to *"booze"* and immediately uses that new word to launch the next rhyme scheme.
- **Oblique Associative Logic:** Non-linear stream-of-consciousness writing. The following bar acknowledges the punchline of the previous bar, but instead of continuing a normal narrative, it shoots off into an esoteric pop-culture, food, or comic-book direction.
- **Supervillain Persona:** Esoteric vocabulary, dry humor, vintage comic book lore, third-person references, internal monologues, and iron-fisted villainy.

### How to Write in This Style
1. Attempt full or near-holorimes: Line 1 and Line 2 matching almost every syllable's vowel sound.
2. Build false expectations: set up a familiar rhyme cadence, then deliberately drop an unexpected, quirky word and pivot.
3. Pack lines with obscure references (Saturday morning cartoons, dusty jazz records, snacks, mechanical contraptions).
4. Speak in the third person (*"DOOM," "The villain"*), embrace eccentric humor, and never deliver a predictable pop structure.
